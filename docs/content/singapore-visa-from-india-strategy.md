### The angle

Two facts that reorder the whole task, and that competing content mentions only in passing:

1. **You cannot lodge it yourself.** Somebody in Singapore submits on your behalf. Finding
   that person or agent is step one, not an administrative detail.
2. **The visa does not set your length of stay.** That is decided at the checkpoint and
   delivered as a digital e-Pass by email, with nothing stamped in the passport.

The arrival-card confusion is the third: it is free, it is not a visa, and a lot of paid sites
depend on people not knowing that. Saying so plainly is a genuine service.

### Deliberate omissions

- **No visa validity or standard length of stay stated.** The ICA page does not publish either,
  and the stay is determined at the checkpoint rather than by the visa. Quoting a duration would
  contradict the article's central point.
- No claim about which agents to use, or their charges.
- No approval rates.

## 6. AEO / GEO

**Direct answer target:** *Can an Indian passport holder apply for a Singapore visa directly, and what does it cost?*

**AI search summary (about sixty words):**

> Holders of ordinary Indian passports need a Singapore entry visa, which cannot be lodged
> directly by the applicant. It is submitted by a local contact in Singapore, a strategic
> partner, an authorised visa agent, or a Singapore Overseas Mission, for a non-refundable
> SGD 30 fee, normally processed within three working days. The period of stay is decided at
> the checkpoint and issued as a digital e-Pass.

**Relationship made explicit:** entry visa → permission to travel, not permission to stay →
checkpoint grants the Visit Pass → e-Pass email is the authoritative record.

## 9. Note on Australia

Australia was researched in the same pass and **not published**. Home Affairs returns 403 to
automated fetches, and the visitor subclass 600 Tourist stream charge could not be pinned
down — one source indicated AUD 150 and another AUD 380, which is exactly the kind of
half-verified figure this content set refuses to print. Australia's charge is composed of a
base charge plus possible additional charges, which is why they publish a pricing estimator
rather than a single number.

When it is written, the honest framing is to name no single fee and route the reader to the
official estimator, the same way the US guide handles appointment wait times.
