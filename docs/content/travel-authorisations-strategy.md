## 1. Why this article exists

It was not on the original plan. It emerged because the same correction had to be written
five times across five country guides - UK ETA, Canada eTA, ETIAS, NZeTA, K-ETA - and a
pattern repeated five times is a pillar page hiding in the cluster.

Consolidating it does three things a country guide cannot:

1. Ranks for the generic query, not just the destination-specific one.
2. Gives the five country guides a natural internal link target.
3. States the durable rule - **authorisations are built for people who did not need a visa** -
   so a reader who meets a sixth scheme next year already knows the answer.

### The hedge carried forward

India's absence from Canada's eTA list is stated as **current and checkable**, exactly as in
`canada-visitor-visa-from-india-strategy.md`. The IRCC page refuses automated fetches, so the
claim rests on two searches and the expansion announcements rather than on reading the list.
The article tells readers to confirm, and says plainly that the day India appears on one of
these lists, the answer for that country changes.

## 4. The taxonomy, which is the actual contribution

Three things get conflated because they all arrive by email:

- **Travel authorisation** - screens people who do not need a visa. Nationality decides.
- **eVisa** - a visa, assessed as one, delivered digitally. Circumstances decide.
- **Conditional visa on arrival** - depends on documents you already hold.

Naming the distinction is more durable than any single eligibility fact, because eligibility
lists move and the distinction does not.
