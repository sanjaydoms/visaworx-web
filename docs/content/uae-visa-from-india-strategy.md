| **Visaworx connection** | Visa Readiness primary; Tourist Visa consultation secondary |

### The angle

The differentiator is that **eligibility depends on documents other than the Indian passport**.
Visa on arrival is granted on the strength of a qualifying US, UK, EU or other residence
permit. This is the single most misunderstood rule in the India-to-UAE corridor, and most
competing content either states "visa on arrival available for Indians" without the condition,
or buries it.

Secondary differentiator: the **February 2025 expansion** added six more countries' residence
permits. Anyone relying on older guidance has an incomplete list.

`Indian passport holder → holds (or does not hold) a qualifying foreign visa or residence
permit → visa on arrival OR sponsored entry permit → GDRFA (Dubai) or ICP (federal) →

## 5. Verified Facts and Citation Ledger

Every claim below is sourced. **Nothing in the article states a figure that is not on this list.**

| Fact | Value | Official source | Verified |
|---|---|---|---|
| VoA eligibility, base | Indian ordinary passport **plus** a valid visa, residence permit or Green Card from the US, UK or EU | GDRFA Dubai; UAE MOFA | 2026-08-21 |
| VoA eligibility, expansion | From **13 February 2025**, valid residence permits of Australia, Canada, Japan, New Zealand, Republic of Korea, Singapore | UAE MOFA, New Delhi mission | 2026-08-21 |
| Passport validity | not less than 6 months | GDRFA Dubai | 2026-08-21 |
| Qualifying document validity | not less than 6 months | GDRFA Dubai | 2026-08-21 |
| Duration | 14 days | GDRFA Dubai | 2026-08-21 |
| Extension | may be extended for a similar period, **one time only** | GDRFA Dubai | 2026-08-21 |
| Non-qualifying route | entry permit arranged in advance through a sponsor (airline, hotel, tour operator, UAE resident or company) | UAE government portal | 2026-08-21 |

### Deliberate omissions

- **No entry-permit fees or durations stated for the non-qualifying route.** These vary by
  permit type and issuing authority and change; the article says so and routes the reader to
  the authority instead. The GDRFA service fee seen during research is deliberately not
  published here, because it applies to one authority's service page and would read as the
  universal price.
- No claim that any route guarantees entry — admission remains the border authority's decision.
- The article states plainly that Dubai and the federal authority differ, rather than
  presenting Dubai's pages as the national rule. This is a common error in competing content.

## 7. GEO Optimization

**AI search summary (about sixty words):**

> Indian passport holders qualify for a UAE visa on arrival only if they hold a valid visa,
> residence permit or Green Card from the United States, United Kingdom or European Union, or
> since February 2025 a residence permit from Australia, Canada, Japan, New Zealand, the
> Republic of Korea or Singapore. It grants 14 days, extendable once. Everyone else needs a
> sponsored entry permit arranged in advance.

**Relationship made explicit:** eligibility is a property of the traveller's *other*
documents, not of the Indian passport. This is the fact the article exists to establish.

## 9. Conversion Strategy

The reader's problem here is upstream of documents: they do not know which of two routes they
are on. The CTA is therefore placed around that question — settle the route before committing
to bookings — rather than around document quality.

## 10. Retrieval Note

The FAQ originally titled "Are UAE visa rules the same for Dubai and the other emirates?" was
reworded to "Does the same UAE entry process apply in Dubai and the other emirates?".

FAQ questions are indexed at weight 3, the highest in the retriever. The word "rules" is filler
in several guard queries used by the assistant test suite ("What are the visa rules for
Madagascar?"), so a FAQ title containing it collided with queries that must return *unsupported*.
Generic filler words — rules, requirements, information, process — are worth avoiding in FAQ
questions for that reason. Prefer the specific noun.
