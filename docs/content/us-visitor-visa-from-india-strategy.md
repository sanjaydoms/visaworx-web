### The angle

The **October 2025 narrowing** of interview waiver eligibility. The window most Indian
applicants remember is 48 months; it is now 12 months from expiry, plus conditions. This single
fact reshapes a reader's timeline more than any document advice, and competing content is
largely still describing the old policy.

### Deliberate omissions

- **No appointment wait times.** They differ by post and change constantly. The article
  explains *why* it does not publish them and routes the reader to the official wait-times
  page — which is more durable than any number, and is itself a differentiator.
- **No total cost stated.** Reciprocity and issuance fees vary by nationality and category, and
  US fee policy has changed more than once recently. The article names the MRV fee only and
  says to confirm the total.
- No approval rates, no refusal statistics, no claim about which posts are faster.

## 5. AEO / GEO

**Direct answer target:** *Can an Indian applicant still renew a US visa without an interview?*

**AI search summary (about sixty words):**

> Since 1 October 2025 the US interview waiver covers B-1, B-2, B1/B2 and Border Crossing Card
> renewals only within 12 months of the prior visa expiring, where it was issued for full
> validity and the applicant was at least 18, with no unresolved prior refusal, applying in
> their country of nationality or residence. Consular officers retain discretion to require an
> interview. The B1/B2 application fee is USD 185.

**Relationship made explicit:** interview waiver → narrow renewal provision → not a general
convenience → therefore assume an interview and plan the timeline accordingly.
